I walked out onto the deck wearing a pair of shorts, a rain jacket unzipped, and flip flops bought for $10 in Nassau. It was late in the night, around 2 AM. It had just been raining so there were large pools of water that I waded through as I walked down the deck. I arrived at the back of the ship and peered over, seeing nothing but darkness, some faint stars, and the frothed water as the engines churned.

If it wasn't for the single rail separating me from tipping into the waters and the powerful winds whipping the hood of my raincoat around and behind my head, it would seem like I was on land.

Yet there is a railing and there are the winds and there is the understanding that if I fell overboard, all too easily I would lose the warmth in my body, energy in my muscles, and hope in my mind as I am left behind and stranded hundreds or thousands of miles away from the safety of land. 

I'd be nothing but a name in a short report while the ocean would continue on, blending beautifully the familiar with the unfamiliar and the sublime with true terror. 

There is life in the ocean. Organisms survive and thrive in this impossibly inhospitable environment that mankind needed to struggle and invent and innovate to begin to traverse. Not to mention that I (human) came from the ocean and in all likelihood will eventually return to it long after my death. On the water, I am simultaneously daring to preside over my origin and my ending. Nothing else that has existed or will be could ever offer the same visceral awe and wonder that the ocean provides. 

At the same time, I cannot help but be in awe that we would have the arrogance to build massive ships, cruise ships, to cater to ordinary people for leisure and entertainment. Rather then tremble in fear or keep a safe distance away, we have decided in all our self-righteousness to shape the universe to our whim. We have decided to build a floating buffet and stuff thousands of people who otherwise would have no right to be in the ocean on a multiday binge of gross excess. Eating, drinking, and sleeping neverendign. As plow through with the might and self-same hubris, I'm again inspired. Tugged on by the same sense of romantic sentiment that countless otherwise before me have felt. Though where they crossed the same waters when they could be killed, I am standing on a multi-billion testament to humanity's remarkable engineering ingenuity.

There is awe and terror. There is also a sense of wistfulness. Perhaps we have gone too far. Become too capable. Come to hold too much power. 

I wonder if awe will survive its conquest. If we can ever return to a romantic world. If in the far future, when humanity has begun to explore the dark reaches of space, if the explorers and wonderers of then will feel the same visceral awe that they did hundreds of years ago. That I feel now traversing safely through the dark.

The terror of the ocean is familiar. Tied through blood. I think space will be different, moreso an exile and a frontier without a home. There will be nothing that remembers us or where we came from. It will be a stranger to be conquered in the routine manner that only a species too accustomed to success can conduct.
