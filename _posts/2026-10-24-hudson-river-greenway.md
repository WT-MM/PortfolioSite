<div class="poem" markdown="1">

There are moments in life
when everything is just open.
Dizzyingly so,
and I can't help but feel numb to it.

And I fall into a deep, deep,
spell

It's oddly periodic.
never consistent,
but always preparing.
I suspect it's busyness, life, inadequacies,
small little feelings of being Out-Of-Place.
Whenever it happens, I try to leave.
It's the only way I can remember
what is so beautiful, so satisfying,
about being alive in this world.

It's the mist-clouded mountaintop at night
without nothing visible
beyond the front windshields.

A silent hill at the end of a trail.

The syncopated traffic lights on an expressway.

Skyscrapers,
lit window by lit window
in some asymmetric pattern
yet falling into the same consistent color.

---

Tonight it's the water.
Just the dark water.

The wind.
The cold.

The cold always makes me feel alive.

Like the rain pattering, pattering
on a metal rooftop.
And it's just cold.
It's wet.

And there's the silence,
but not a true silence
because there's always music.
There's always music of some kind.

Music is such a —

There's nothing better than a cold reminder
of how wonderful it is to be alive.

---

It's the blurry street lamps on the lawns of campus,
and you're too dizzy to stand,
and you can't do anything,
can't help but lie on the grass
as it turns ever later into the night,
and ever colder.

That's good.
Because the cold is reminiscent of life.

It's the pitch-black sky,
speckled with small, pinpricks,
countless stars.

It's the silence of the beach,
backlit by the view of humanity's grand achievements.
Of the achievements yet to be accomplished.

It's the feeling of walking amidst empty streets,
in the early morning
or the late night.

---

The one thing it isn't is people.
The indoors.
Home.

That's the only thing it can't be.
Because all of a sudden you forget about the world,
and you forget about life —

and then you're brought back.

Back out of the cold.
But really it's into the cold.

Because it's just the cold.

The emptiness is just the emptiness.

Yeah. That makes sense.

</div>
